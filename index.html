        About

        Creator

        Created by Noah Rutledge as a prototype for a clothing brand's social-engagement campaign aimed at US college students.

        Weather data

        Weather comes from Open-Meteo. For US locations its forecasts are based on NOAA's Global Forecast System (GFS) model. The app shows today and the next 7 days, with "feels like" temperatures in °F and wind in mph.

        Recommendation rules

        One recommendation drives the character, icons, text and reminders. Each day gets:

        • A condition, first match wins: Snowy (any snowfall), Rainy (30% or higher chance of precipitation), Windy (wind of 20 mph or more), Cloudy (average cloud cover of 60% or more), otherwise Clear. "Clear" means none of the others apply, so a partly cloudy day still counts as Clear.
        • A temperature band from the average of the day's low and high "feels like" temperature: Hot (85°F or above), Warm (70–84), Mild (55–69), Cool (40–54), Cold (below 40).
        • Reminders, each independent: Umbrella (30% or higher chance of precipitation), Sunscreen (UV index 3 or higher, hidden when precipitation chance is 60% or higher), and Extra hydration (a "feels like" high of 90°F or above).

        Basis: a UV index of 3 is "Moderate" on the US EPA/NWS UV index scale. The hydration threshold sits near the start of the "Extreme Caution" level on the NWS heat index chart; Open-Meteo has no heat index, so its "feels like" temperature stands in for it. Dressing in layers follows REI's layering basics. The temperature bands, the 30% rain threshold and the 60% cloud threshold are judgment calls, not published standards, so treat the advice as a suggestion.

        Privacy

        • No accounts, no analytics, and no server of our own. This is a static site.
        • Your most recent location is saved in this browser only. So are the random outfit and wording choices for each date, which keep a day looking the same when you come back. Nothing else is stored.
        • Your device location is requested only when you press "Use my device location." Its coordinates go to Open-Meteo to fetch the forecast, and are not saved as a place name.
        • Open-Meteo is the one outside party that receives data: it logs your IP address and the coordinates you request for up to 90 days for technical and anti-abuse purposes, and does not share them with third parties. See the Open-Meteo terms and privacy.
